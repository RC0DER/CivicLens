    Protection in force

    Four separations

    1. Identity from report. Contact details, where given, sit in a separate database keyed by a one-way token. The case file has no column to hold them.
    2. Report from department. Officials of the accused office see the allegation and the timeline. The service that answers them holds no key to the contact store.
    3. Investigator from office. Cases route to an investigator outside the department complained against — refused in code, not by policy.
    4. Outcome from silence. Every status change is written to the public ledger, whether or not it favours the department.

    About the platform

    Why the register is public

    A grievance that disappears into a departmental queue teaches citizens that reporting is futile. CivicLens inverts the default: the complaint is public from the moment it is filed, and the complainant is private forever.

    Publication is what creates pressure. A department cannot quietly shelve a case whose status line is visible to every journalist in the city, and an investigator who lets the statutory 14-day assignment window lapse does so on the record — the breach is published to the ledger automatically.

    Limits you should know

    What this platform is not

    It is not an emergency service. If you are being threatened, call 112 first. It does not adjudicate service complaints such as delayed water connections — those belong with the municipal grievance cell. And it cannot compel prosecution: it refers substantiated findings to the prosecuting authority and publishes what happens next.

    It also cannot protect you from an observer watching your own connection. If that is your risk, use a device and network that are not yours.
