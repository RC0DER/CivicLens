Protection in force

Four separations

  1. Identity from report. Contact details, where given, sit in a separate store keyed by a one-way token. The case file has no field to hold them.
  2. Report from department. Officials of the accused office see the allegation and the timeline; they never see the intake record.
  3. Investigator from office. Cases route to an investigator outside the department complained against. The code is public; the name is not.
  4. Outcome from silence. Every status change is written to the public ledger within 24 hours, whether or not it favours the department.

About the platform

Why the register is public

A grievance that disappears into a departmental queue teaches citizens that reporting is futile. CivicLens inverts the default: the complaint is public from the moment it is filed, and the complainant is private forever.

Publication is what creates pressure. A department cannot quietly shelve a case whose status line is visible to every journalist in the city, and an investigator who lets the statutory 14-day assignment window lapse does so on the record.

Limits you should know

What this platform is not

It is not an emergency service. If you are being threatened, call 112 first. It does not adjudicate service complaints such as delayed water connections — those belong with the municipal grievance cell. And it cannot compel prosecution: it refers substantiated findings to the prosecuting authority and publishes what happens next.
